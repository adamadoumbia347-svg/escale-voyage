Sending a parcel or extra bags home

Extra checked bag, postal parcel, air freight, shared sea container: which option to choose depending on weight, urgency and budget, and the rules to know before you send.

In short

  • You are travelling and it is one or two suitcases: extra baggage, bought online before departure.
  • Small urgent parcel (documents, permitted medicines, a phone): the post office or an express courier.
  • A few boxes: air freight, priced per kilo, in a few days.
  • Large, non-urgent volume (furniture, appliances, barrels): a shared sea container, in several weeks.

1. The extra bag on the plane

  • Buy the extra bag online before departure: it is usually cheaper than at the airport desk.
  • Compare the fare with bags included rather than the bare fare: a dearer airline can end up cheaper once the suitcases are counted.
  • Weigh everything at home: an extra kilo is expensive at check-in.
  • Each airline's allowance and rules: baggage by airline and the guide how not to pay more.

2. Postal or express parcel

  • For a small parcel, your country's postal service (Royal Mail in the UK, La Poste in France, Poste Italiane in Italy, bpost in Belgium) or an express courier.
  • Get the quote online with the real weight and dimensions: the price also depends on volume.
  • Fill in the customs declaration carefully (contents, value) and keep the tracking number.
  • Ask whether delivery is to the door or to the post office: in many cities the recipient has to collect the parcel.

3. Air freight

  • Freight agents specialised by country collect boxes in Europe and hand them over on arrival, often at their own office.
  • The price is calculated per kilo or by volume: always ask for a written quote, with what is included (packing, customs, delivery).
  • Check that the agent has an address, a company number and a receipt listing the contents.

4. Shared sea container

  • For barrels, many boxes, furniture and appliances: you pay for a share of a container.
  • Allow several weeks, plus customs clearance on arrival.
  • Make a detailed, photographed inventory: it is used by customs and in case of loss or damage.

What you should not send

  • Loose lithium batteries, aerosols, flammable products and perfume in quantity are restricted or banned on aircraft.
  • Cash, valuable jewellery and original identity documents do not travel in an ordinary parcel.
  • Medicines, food, plants and animal products have rules specific to each country.
  • On arrival, it is the destination country's customs that decides: check its rules or ask the freight agent before sending.

Frequently asked questions

Extra suitcase or parcel: which is cheaper?

For one or two suitcases, extra baggage bought online before departure is often the simplest, since you travel with it. Beyond that, or if you are not travelling, ask for a freight quote: the price is per kilo or by volume and depends on the country.

How long does sea freight take?

Several weeks, plus customs clearance on arrival. Sea freight suits bulky, non-urgent belongings. Ask the agent for a door-to-door time, not just the crossing time.

Will there be customs duties on arrival?

Often yes: each country has its own rules for new goods, electronics, vehicles or commercial quantities. Ask the destination country's customs or the freight agent before sending, so you do not discover the charges at collection.

Can I give a parcel to a traveller?

It is common practice, but risky for both sides: the traveller is responsible for everything they carry before customs and the airline. Only hand over or accept open parcels whose contents are known and allowed.
