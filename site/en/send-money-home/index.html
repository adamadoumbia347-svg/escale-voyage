Sending money back home

Wise, Remitly, WorldRemit, Sendwave, Taptap Send, Western Union, MoneyGram… Rates change every day. Here is how to compare in two minutes and stop losing money in the exchange rate.

In short

  • Do not compare the advertised fees: compare the amount the person receives for the same amount sent.
  • The cost is often hidden in the exchange rate, even when the service says “zero fees”.
  • The way the money is received changes the price: mobile money, bank account or cash pick-up.
  • Use a free public comparison site before every large transfer.

The method in 4 steps

  1. Decide how much to send and how the person wants to receive it (mobile money, account, cash).
  2. Open two or three services and enter exactly the same amount and the same country.
  3. For each one, note the amount received in the local currency and the delivery time shown.
  4. Pick the one that delivers the most within a delay that suits you. Compare again next time: the ranking changes.

Public comparison sites

These sites compare transfer services without selling anything:

  • Remittance Prices Worldwide: the World Bank database, country by country.
  • envoidargent.fr: comparison site backed by the French development agency, for transfers from France (in French).
  • mandasoldiacasa.it: Italian comparison site for transfers from Italy.

The best-known services

Links to the official sites, in no particular order: Escale earns no commission on these services.

  • Wise: transfer to a bank account, and mobile money in some countries.
  • Remitly: bank account, mobile money or cash pick-up depending on the country.
  • WorldRemit: mobile money, bank account, cash, airtime top-up.
  • Sendwave: mobile app, mostly to mobile money.
  • Taptap Send: mobile app, mostly to mobile money.
  • Western Union and MoneyGram: agent networks for cash pick-up, plus online transfers.

Not every service covers every country or every mobile money operator: check on their site before opening an account.

Traps to avoid

  • Sending several small amounts: fixed fees can add up. Check whether a single transfer costs less.
  • Paying by credit card: some services or banks add fees. A bank transfer or debit card often costs less.
  • Getting one digit wrong in the recipient's number: a transfer to the wrong number is very hard to recover. Read it twice.
  • Forgetting the limits: each service caps amounts and may ask for ID or proof of funds.

What about the plane ticket?

A relative back home can sometimes pay for the ticket directly: see paying for a flight with Orange Money, Wave or MTN MoMo. For money during the trip: money abroad.

Frequently asked questions

Which service is cheapest for sending money to Africa?

There is no fixed answer: it depends on the country, the amount, how the money is received and the day. The right habit is to compare, for the same amount sent, what the person will actually receive. The public comparison sites listed on this page do that calculation for free.

Why look at the exchange rate and not only the fees?

Because part of the cost is often hidden in the exchange rate. A service can advertise “zero fees” and apply a worse rate. Only the amount received, in the local currency, lets you compare.

Mobile money, bank account or cash pick-up?

Mobile money (Orange Money, Wave, MTN MoMo…) is handy when the recipient has no bank account and lives far from an agent. A bank transfer suits large amounts. Cash pick-up is still useful in an emergency, but needs ID and a trip to the agent.

How do I avoid scams?

Only send money to people you know. Be wary of invented emergencies, promised winnings and requests to keep the transfer secret. Always go through the service's official site or app, never through a link received by message.
